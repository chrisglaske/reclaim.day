How to track your smoking and cut back

Whether you want to quit, cut down or just see what’s going on, writing it down is a good first step.

Most people who want to smoke less already have a rough idea of how much they smoke. A log turns that guess into something you can look at. It isn’t about judging yourself. It’s about noticing.

1. Start by just counting

For a few days, note each cigarette when you smoke it. Don’t set a goal yet and don’t change anything. You’re collecting information about a normal day.

Logging in the moment is easier than remembering later. Reclaim’s quick log takes a tap, so it doesn’t get in the way.

2. Look for the patterns

The number matters less than the when and why. A few things worth noticing:

  • The time of day, such as first thing, after meals or late at night.
  • The place or the activity, like coffee, driving, or a break at work.
  • How you were feeling, whether stressed, bored, social or tired.

Once you can see the moments that come up again and again, you can plan for them instead of being caught off guard.

3. Choose a pace that fits you

There’s no single right way. Some people stop all at once. Some cut down a little each week. Some only want to track for now, and that counts too. You can change your mind at any point.

If you do want a limit, make it one you can actually keep. A small goal you reach teaches you more than a big one you drop.

4. Ride out the strong moments

Cravings tend to come in waves that rise and then pass. It can help to have a few things ready, like a glass of water, a short walk or a few slow breaths, so you aren’t working out what to do in the middle of one.

5. Be kind about the hard days

A day with more cigarettes than you hoped isn’t a failure. It’s information about what made that day hard. Note it, and carry on. The log is there to help you, not to grade you.
